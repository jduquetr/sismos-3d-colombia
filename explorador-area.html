Modeled top of the subducting slab (Slab2, Hayes et al. 2018). On the 2D map it draws USGS's depth contours worldwide. In 3D it builds the slab over the queried area from those contours, clipped to the box's depth range — where the model has no data, there's a hole.
Slab opacity (volume + contours) 35%
From a surface to a volume.
Slab2 publishes only the top of the slab: a surface, with no thickness. Giving it a floor turns it into a body you can see the seismicity fall inside of.

The default 10 km is an assumption, not data: the average thickness of oceanic crust. It's the crust alone — the subducting lithosphere, crust plus its mantle, is several tens of km thick — so treat the volume as the crustal part of the slab, and change the number if your case calls for another one.

The floor is offset perpendicular to the slab, not straight down. A vertical offset would read as 10 km only where the slab is flat, and would inflate to 10/cos(dip) as it steepens — about 14 km at 45°. Measuring perpendicular keeps the thickness constant no matter the dip.

Each sphere's radius follows the earthquake's instrumental magnitude. At 100% the radius is proportional to the expected rupture length (100.5M), so an M7 is about 30 times an M4 — true to the physics, but it swamps the scene when there are many events. At 0% every sphere is the same size. This applies to both layers and to the 2D map; each layer keeps its own overall scale.

Significant events by year
The same listing as USGS's Browse significant earthquakes: any event whose significance (sig, which combines magnitude, felt reports and PAGER alert level) is above 600 — not a magnitude cutoff. Years 1900 to today, as on their page. Picking a year queries the USGS catalog live; picking an event loads it with the seismicity around it.
How it is added
With this on, loading a special event leaves the current plot untouched and draws the new earthquakes over it, clipped to the area of the search already on screen — anything outside that area is left out, since the scene is built for it. With it off, the event brings its own regional context and replaces what is plotted.
